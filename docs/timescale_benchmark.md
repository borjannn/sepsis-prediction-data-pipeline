## 5. Interpretation (generated from the measurements above)

- Storage: the columnstore hypertable is 55.2 MB vs 938.5 MB for the plain table (17.0x smaller, including indexes).
- segmentby patient_key compresses worse (65.6 MB) than segmentby variable_key (55.2 MB).
- Q1 (One patient's full trajectory): columnstore is 46.2x slower than the plain table; rowstore hypertable is 7.1x slower than the plain table.
- Q2 (Early window: per-variable average, stay hours 1-24): columnstore is 5.1x faster than the plain table; rowstore hypertable is 1.2x faster than the plain table.
- Q3 (Late window: per-variable average, stay hours 168-336): columnstore is 76.4x faster than the plain table; rowstore hypertable is 12.4x faster than the plain table.
- Q4 (Average HR per 6-hour bucket over all patients): columnstore is 6.6x faster than the plain table; rowstore hypertable is 1.2x slower than the plain table.
- Q5 (One variable (lactate) across all patients): columnstore is 11.9x faster than the plain table; rowstore hypertable is 1.4x slower than the plain table.
- Q4 from the continuous aggregate is 3.1x faster than the plain table.

Structural context: the dataset has relative time only, so Timescale's wall-clock features (retention policies, real-time aggregation of newly arriving data) do not apply, and a patient's rows span several chunks, so time partitioning cannot help per-patient access. This is why TimescaleDB is an isolated add-on, not the core of the warehouse.
